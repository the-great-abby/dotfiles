# Interactive Stress Recovery Runbook

A compassionate, systematic approach to recovering from overwhelm, crisis, or burnout when your productivity systems have broken down. Focuses on gentle rebuilding, stress management, and creating sustainable systems that prevent future breakdowns.

## Purpose

This runbook helps you:
- **Assess current stress and overwhelm levels** with compassionate self-awareness
- **Stabilize immediate crisis situations** with emergency prioritization
- **Rebuild productivity systems gradually** without additional overwhelm
- **Process accumulated mental and emotional load** systematically
- **Create sustainable practices** that prevent future system breakdowns
- **Reintegrate with GTD workflow** at a manageable pace
- **Build resilience and early warning systems** for future stress management

## Prerequisites

1. **Recognition that systems need rebuilding** - You're feeling overwhelmed or systems have broken down
2. **Willingness to start small** - Focus on gentle progress rather than dramatic fixes
3. **Self-compassion** - Understanding that system breakdowns are normal and recoverable
4. **Time for gradual rebuilding** - This is a process, not a quick fix

## Instructions for AI Agent

This is a compassionate and gradual runbook. Ask **ONE question at a time**, validate difficult feelings, and focus on small sustainable steps rather than overwhelming changes. Prioritize emotional safety and gentle progress over productivity optimization.

### Phase 1: Current State Assessment & Stabilization

**Step 1: Overwhelm Assessment**

```
🤗 **Welcome to Your Interactive Stress Recovery Session!**

First, let's acknowledge that needing system recovery is completely normal and shows self-awareness, not failure.

**Question 1:** How are you feeling right now, and what's brought you to needing stress recovery?

**Current State:**
• **Stress Level:** On a scale of 1-10, how stressed/overwhelmed do you feel?
• **System Status:** How broken down are your usual productivity systems? (completely, partially, just struggling)
• **Duration:** How long have you been feeling this way? (days, weeks, months)
• **Trigger:** What started this period of overwhelm? (work crisis, personal event, gradual buildup)

**Physical & Emotional Symptoms:**
Are you experiencing any of these?
• **Physical:** Fatigue, sleep issues, tension, appetite changes, getting sick more often
• **Mental:** Difficulty focusing, memory issues, racing thoughts, decision paralysis
• **Emotional:** Anxiety, irritability, sadness, feeling disconnected, guilt about productivity
• **Behavioral:** Avoiding tasks, procrastination, social withdrawal, abandoning routines

**Immediate Safety:**
• Are you safe and do you have basic needs met?
• Any immediate crises that need attention first?
• Support people available to help you?

This assessment helps us understand where to start your recovery gently and effectively.

[Assess current state with compassion and identify immediate needs]
```

**Step 2: Crisis Triage & Immediate Stabilization**

```
**Question 2:** Let's identify what absolutely must be handled immediately vs. what can wait for recovery.

**Emergency Triage:**
🚨 **Immediate (Today/This Week):**
• **Health/Safety:** Anything affecting physical safety or urgent health needs?
• **Legal/Financial:** Deadlines with serious consequences (bills, legal, taxes)?
• **Relationship:** Critical communications needed to maintain important relationships?
• **Work:** Only things that would cause job loss or serious professional damage?

⏰ **Soon (Next 2 Weeks):**
• **Important but not emergency:** Things that matter but won't cause crisis if delayed slightly
• **Communication:** People who need to know you're taking time to recover
• **Basic Maintenance:** Minimum needed to keep life functioning

📋 **Can Wait (Recovery Period):**
• **Optimization:** Making things better vs. keeping them functioning
• **New Projects:** Anything not already in progress
• **Non-Essential:** Things that feel urgent but aren't truly important

**Reality Check:**
Looking at your "immediate" list:
• **Is this really emergency-level?** Or does stress make everything feel urgent?
• **What would happen if this waited one more week?** Often the consequence is less than overwhelm tells us.
• **Can anyone else handle this?** Who could help with immediate items?

**Stabilization Actions:**
• What are the 1-3 most important things to handle this week?
• What can you delegate, postpone, or eliminate entirely?
• Who can you ask for help or extensions?

[Identify true emergencies and create breathing room for recovery]
```

### Phase 2: Mental & Emotional Load Processing

**Step 3: Brain Dump & Worry Processing**

```
**Question 3:** Let's get everything out of your head in a safe, organized way.

**Complete Brain Dump:**
Without worrying about organization, let's capture everything that's on your mind:

**Work Concerns:**
• Tasks you're worried about
• Projects that feel overwhelming  
• Decisions you need to make
• People you need to respond to
• Deadlines that are stressing you

**Personal Concerns:**
• Family or relationship issues
• Health worries
• Financial concerns
• Home/life maintenance needs
• Social obligations

**Internal Worries:**
• Things you're criticizing yourself for
• Fears about the future
• Regrets or guilt about the past
• Self-doubt or confidence issues

**"Should" List:**
• Things you feel you "should" be doing
• Expectations (your own or others')
• Comparisons to others or past self

**Processing Questions:**
Looking at everything you've captured:
• **How does it feel to have it all written down?**
• **Any surprise about how much/little is actually there?**
• **What themes or patterns do you notice?**
• **What feels most emotionally charged vs. practically manageable?**

**Emotional Validation:**
This is a lot to carry in your head. No wonder you feel overwhelmed. Having it external where you can see it is the first step to making it manageable.

[Complete mental download and initial emotional processing]
```

**Step 4: Worry Categorization & Reality Testing**

```
**Question 4:** Let's organize your concerns and reality-test what's actually within your control.

**Worry Categories:**

**🎯 Actionable & In My Control:**
• Things you can actually do something about
• Decisions that are yours to make
• Actions you can take

**👥 Actionable & Requires Others:**
• Things you can influence but need cooperation
• Conversations you can initiate
• Requests you can make

**🌊 Not Actionable / Out of My Control:**
• Other people's choices or reactions
• Past events that can't be changed
• Future scenarios that may never happen
• External circumstances beyond your influence

**🧠 Internal Work Needed:**
• Thought patterns or beliefs to examine
• Emotional processing needed
• Self-care or healing required
• Mindset shifts that would help

**Reality Testing Questions:**
For items in your "actionable" categories:
• **What specifically can you do?** (vs. vague worry)
• **When does this actually need attention?** (vs. stress timeline)
• **What's the real consequence if this isn't perfect?** (vs. catastrophic thinking)
• **Who could help you with this?** (vs. doing everything alone)

**Control Shift:**
• What feels lighter when you recognize it's not your responsibility to control?
• What feels more manageable when you identify specific actions?
• What needs emotional processing rather than problem-solving?

[Organize worries by actionability and reality-test overwhelm thoughts]
```

### Phase 3: System Rebuilding Strategy

**Step 5: Gentle System Assessment**

```
**Question 5:** Let's assess what's working, what's broken, and what needs gentle rebuilding.

**System Status Check:**

**GTD System Elements:**
Rate each area (Working Well / Partially Broken / Completely Broken):

• **Inbox/Capture:** Are you capturing new things or letting them pile up in your head?
• **Task Lists:** Current, overwhelming, abandoned, or non-existent?
• **Project Organization:** Clear projects or everything feels like chaos?
• **Calendar:** Reliable or overscheduled/underscheduled?
• **Reviews:** When did you last do a weekly review?
• **Contexts:** Still using contexts or everything mixed together?

**Habit Systems:**
• **Morning Routine:** Functioning or disrupted?
• **Evening Routine:** Helping or non-existent?
• **Exercise:** Maintaining or dropped?
• **Sleep:** Consistent or disrupted?
• **Nutrition:** Taking care of yourself or survival mode?

**Support Systems:**
• **Professional Support:** Colleagues, managers, team support available?
• **Personal Support:** Family, friends, community connections?
• **Self-Care:** Practices that restore vs. deplete you?

**What's Still Working:**
• What systems or habits are still functioning even during this difficult time?
• What's been your anchor or stability?
• What are you proud of maintaining?

**Rebuilding Priority:**
• Which broken system causes the most additional stress?
• What would make the biggest difference if it was working again?
• What feels most manageable to start rebuilding first?

[Assess system status with focus on what's working and gentle rebuilding priorities]
```

**Step 6: Minimal Viable System Design**

```
**Question 6:** Let's design the simplest possible system that will support you without adding overwhelm.

**Minimal Viable GTD:**

**Simple Capture:**
Instead of complex systems, what's one easy way to capture new items?
• **Phone notes app** for quick capture?
• **Single notebook** that stays with you?
• **One email folder** for everything that needs attention?
• **Voice memos** when writing feels like too much?

**Basic Task Management:**
What's the simplest task system that would actually help?
• **Three lists:** Today, This Week, Someday
• **Two lists:** Must Do, When I Can
• **One list:** Next Actions (just the very next step for everything)
• **Paper system:** Simple notebook or index cards

**Essential Reviews:**
What's the minimum review that would prevent things from falling through cracks?
• **Daily 5-minute check:** What must happen today?
• **Weekly 15-minute review:** What needs attention this week?
• **No formal review:** Just handle things as they come up for now

**Calendar Basics:**
• **Appointments only:** Don't try to time-block everything
• **Buffer time:** Space between commitments for overwhelm management
• **Recovery blocks:** Time specifically protected for rest/processing

**Support Integration:**
• **One person** you check in with regularly about your recovery
• **Simple communication** to others about your reduced capacity
• **Basic self-care** built into your minimal system

**What feels manageable?**
• Which minimal system elements feel supportive vs. burdensome?
• What would you actually use vs. what you think you "should" do?
• How can we make this even simpler if needed?

[Design sustainable minimal system that supports without overwhelming]
```

### Phase 4: Gradual Capacity Rebuilding

**Step 7: Energy and Capacity Assessment**

```
**Question 7:** Let's honestly assess your current capacity and build from there.

**Current Capacity Inventory:**

**Daily Energy:**
On a typical day right now:
• **Morning Energy:** What do you have capacity for in the morning? (1-3 things, light tasks, just getting started)
• **Afternoon Energy:** Mid-day capacity? (maintaining morning energy, second wind, crash time)
• **Evening Energy:** Energy for personal life? (family time, self-care, planning)

**Weekly Rhythm:**
• **Good Days:** Which days of the week feel more manageable?
• **Difficult Days:** When do you consistently struggle more?
• **Weekend Recovery:** Are weekends restorative or more stressful?

**Task Capacity:**
Right now, what feels manageable?
• **Number:** How many tasks can you reasonably handle per day? (1-2, 3-5, more)
• **Complexity:** Simple tasks only, or some complex work?
• **Duration:** Short tasks, or can you sustain focus for longer periods?
• **Interaction:** Comfortable with people interactions or need solo work?

**Overwhelm Triggers:**
What consistently pushes you over your current capacity?
• **Too many decisions** in one day
• **Unexpected requests** or changes
• **Complex problem-solving** when already tired
• **Social interaction** when you need quiet
• **Physical demands** when energy is low

**Capacity Building:**
• What would slightly increase your capacity without stress?
• What support would help you handle more without overwhelm?
• What recovery practices help restore capacity?

[Assess realistic current capacity and identify gentle building approaches]
```

**Step 8: Progressive Task Reintegration**

```
**Question 8:** Let's gradually reintegrate tasks and responsibilities at a sustainable pace.

**Reintegration Strategy:**

**Week 1-2: Foundation**
What are the absolute basics needed for life stability?
• **Personal care:** Minimum self-care, sleep, nutrition
• **Critical communications:** Essential work or personal communications
• **Basic maintenance:** Minimum needed to keep life functioning
• **One small win:** Something that would feel good to accomplish

**Week 3-4: Core Expansion**
Building on foundation:
• **Key work responsibilities:** Most important professional tasks
• **Important relationships:** Quality time with people who matter most
• **System basics:** Simple capture and task management
• **Gentle challenges:** Slightly stretch current capacity

**Month 2: System Integration**
Adding structure back gradually:
• **Regular reviews:** Weekly check-ins on progress and needs
• **Project work:** Begin working on important longer-term items
• **Optimization:** Small improvements to systems and routines
• **Social reintegration:** Gradually expand social and professional engagement

**Pacing Guidelines:**
• **50% rule:** Only schedule 50% of what you think you can handle
• **Recovery time:** Build in recovery time between demanding tasks
• **Flexibility:** Be ready to scale back if overwhelm returns
• **Celebration:** Acknowledge progress, even small steps

**Warning Signs:**
What would indicate you're pushing too hard too fast?
• Physical symptoms returning (fatigue, tension, sleep issues)
• Emotional overwhelm increasing
• Systems starting to break down again
• Avoiding tasks or people
• Self-criticism increasing

**Adjustment Protocol:**
If warning signs appear:
• Immediately scale back to previous sustainable level
• Add more recovery time
• Simplify current commitments
• Check in with support people

[Create progressive, sustainable reintegration plan]
```

### Phase 5: Resilience Building & Future Prevention

**Step 9: Stress Pattern Analysis**

```
**Question 9:** Let's identify patterns that led to overwhelm and create early warning systems.

**Overwhelm Pattern Analysis:**

**Buildup Pattern:**
Looking back at what led to this breakdown:
• **Early warning signs:** What were the first indicators things were getting difficult?
• **Escalation points:** When did manageable stress become overwhelming?
• **Ignored signals:** What body/mind/emotional signals did you overlook?
• **System breakdown:** At what point did your systems stop working?

**Contributing Factors:**
What made you more vulnerable to overwhelm?
• **Overcommitment:** Saying yes to too much
• **Perfectionism:** Setting unrealistic standards
• **Boundary issues:** Not protecting time or energy
• **Support gaps:** Trying to handle everything alone
• **Self-care neglect:** Not maintaining physical/emotional wellbeing
• **External stressors:** Life circumstances beyond your control

**Personal Stress Triggers:**
What consistently pushes you toward overwhelm?
• **Work situations:** Deadlines, difficult people, complex projects
• **Personal situations:** Family stress, health issues, financial pressure
• **Internal triggers:** Self-criticism, comparison, catastrophic thinking
• **Environmental factors:** Clutter, noise, schedule chaos

**Protective Factors:**
What helps you stay resilient?
• **Relationships:** People who support and energize you
• **Practices:** Activities that restore and center you
• **Boundaries:** Limits that protect your wellbeing
• **Systems:** Organizational approaches that reduce mental load

**Early Warning System:**
What signs would indicate you need to take preventive action?
• **Physical:** Fatigue, tension, sleep changes
• **Emotional:** Increased anxiety, irritability, sadness
• **Behavioral:** Avoiding tasks, isolating, abandoning routines
• **Systemic:** Tasks piling up, missing appointments, falling behind

[Identify overwhelm patterns and create early warning systems]
```

**Step 10: Resilience System Design**

```
**Question 10:** Let's build systems that prevent future overwhelm and support ongoing resilience.

**Resilience Infrastructure:**

**Boundaries & Limits:**
• **Capacity limits:** Clear understanding of your sustainable workload
• **Time boundaries:** Protected time for rest, relationships, and recovery
• **Energy boundaries:** Limits on energy-draining activities and people
• **Communication boundaries:** How and when people can reach you

**Regular Maintenance:**
• **Daily practices:** Small daily actions that maintain wellbeing
• **Weekly reviews:** Regular check-ins on stress levels and system health
• **Monthly assessments:** Broader view of life balance and goal alignment
• **Seasonal adjustments:** Adapting systems to changing life circumstances

**Support Network:**
• **Professional support:** Colleagues, mentors, or professionals who help with work stress
• **Personal support:** Friends, family, or community for emotional support
• **Accountability partners:** People who help you maintain healthy boundaries
• **Emergency support:** Who to contact when overwhelm starts building

**Stress Management Toolkit:**
**Immediate stress relief (0-5 minutes):**
• Breathing exercises, brief walks, stretching
• Quick grounding techniques, positive self-talk

**Short-term stress management (15-60 minutes):**
• Exercise, meditation, creative activities
• Nature time, connecting with supportive people

**Long-term resilience building:**
• Regular exercise routine, consistent sleep schedule
• Meaningful relationships, purpose-aligned work
• Continuous learning, personal growth practices

**System Health Indicators:**
How will you monitor your system health ongoing?
• **Green flags:** Signs that systems are working well
• **Yellow flags:** Early warning that attention is needed
• **Red flags:** Immediate action required to prevent breakdown

**Preventive Actions:**
What will you do when you notice yellow flags?
• Reduce commitments temporarily
• Increase self-care and support
• Review and adjust systems
• Seek help before crisis point

[Design comprehensive resilience and prevention systems]
```
